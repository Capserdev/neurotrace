How to use NeuroTrace

A step-by-step guide to completing your neurological screening — from setup to reading your report.

Start Screening →
0
SETUP

Connect to the backend

Go to the Assessment page and click Test Connection at the top. You should see "Connected (7 models loaded)" in green. If it fails, the AI backend may be waking up — wait 30 seconds and try again.

Good to know
The backend runs on Hugging Face Spaces and may take up to 60 seconds to wake up after a period of inactivity.
All 7 models must be loaded before uploads are enabled.
1
STEP 1 — VOICE

Record your voice

You'll record three short clips. For each one, find a quiet room, position your mouth about 10–15 cm from the microphone, and follow the on-screen instructions.

The three recordings
Sustained "Ahh" — Take a deep breath and say "ahhhh" steadily for 5–7 seconds at a comfortable pitch. Try not to run out of breath.
Vowel repetition — Repeat "ah, eh, ee" clearly and evenly, at a normal speaking pace.
Connected speech — Read the sentence displayed on screen aloud at your natural pace.
Avoid background noise, fan hum, or music — acoustic features are sensitive to environmental sound.
2
STEP 2 — DRAWING

Upload your drawings

Draw each pattern on plain white paper with a dark pen or marker. Take a clear, well-lit photo (no glare, no shadows) and upload it. You'll do this for up to three drawing types.

Wave
Smooth sinusoidal wave
Spiral
Archimedean spiral outward
Meander
Grid of horizontal & vertical lines
Tips for best results
Use your dominant hand and draw at your natural, relaxed pace — don't try to compensate for any tremor.
Draw on unlined white A4 or letter-size paper. Avoid ruled or graph paper.
Photograph the drawing flat on a desk in good lighting. The photo should fill most of the frame.
JPG or PNG both work. File size under 10 MB.
3
STEP 3 — SURVEY

Complete the lifestyle survey

The survey consists of clinically-validated questions about motor symptoms, non-motor indicators, and lifestyle risk factors. Answer honestly based on your experience over the past few months — there are no right or wrong answers.

What it covers
Motor symptoms: tremor, rigidity, slowness of movement, balance issues.
Non-motor signs: sleep disturbances, smell changes, mood, fatigue.
Risk factors: age, family history, exposure history.
Survey responses stay in your browser — nothing is transmitted to the server.
4
STEP 4 — REPORT

Read your ensemble report

Once all steps are complete, head to the Report tab. The ensemble model combines all modalities into a single 4-class probability distribution across HC, PD, PSP, and MSA.

How to read the report
High confidence (≥70%) — strong signal in one class. Moderate (50–69%) — notable lean but not definitive. Low (<50%) — uncertain, consider repeating.
The per-model breakdown shows each model's individual prediction so you can see which modalities drove the result.
More modalities completed = more accurate ensemble. Try to complete all three steps for best results.
Important: A result indicating PD, PSP, or MSA is not a diagnosis — it is a prompt to seek professional evaluation. Always consult a qualified neurologist before drawing any conclusions.

Before you start — checklist

Make sure you have everything ready for an accurate assessment.

Quiet room No background music, fan noise, or conversation nearby for the voice recording.
Pen & white paper A dark pen or marker and unlined white paper for the drawing tasks.
Camera or scanner To photograph your drawings clearly. Good lighting, no glare.
About 5 minutes The full assessment takes roughly 5 minutes from start to report.
Working microphone Built-in laptop mic works fine; a headset gives cleaner audio.
Stable internet Audio and image uploads require a working connection to the API backend.
