Frequently Asked Questions

Common questions about NeuroTrace and how it works.

NeuroTrace is an AI-powered research screening tool that combines three modalities — handwriting analysis, vocal biomarkers, and a lifestyle survey — to assess your risk for Parkinson's Disease and related conditions (PSP, MSA). It was developed by researchers affiliated with Yale University and Mayo Clinic and has been accepted for presentation at the 2026 Movement Disorders Society Congress.

No. NeuroTrace is a research screening tool, not a diagnostic device. It has not been evaluated or approved by the FDA. Results should never be used as the sole basis for a medical decision. If your results concern you, please consult a qualified neurologist or healthcare professional.

The seven underlying models average 89% accuracy on their respective validation sets. The handwriting models (EfficientNetV2B0) and voice classifiers (SVC) were trained and validated on separate held-out datasets. However, real-world performance varies — factors like recording quality, drawing conditions, and individual variability all affect reliability.

You need a device with a microphone and camera or image upload capability. For the voice step, record yourself saying "ahh" for a few seconds in a quiet environment. For the drawing step, draw a spiral, meander, or wave pattern on paper, take a clear photo, and upload it. The survey requires no special equipment.

Parkinson's Disease causes micrographia (small, cramped writing) and tremor, which appear as irregular spacing, waviness, and inconsistent stroke width in drawings. Our EfficientNetV2B0 vision models are trained on thousands of spiral, meander, and wave drawings from both healthy individuals and PD patients to detect these subtle motor-control signatures.

PD affects the muscles controlling speech, causing measurable changes in vocal fold vibration. Our models extract 36 acoustic features — including jitter (frequency variation), shimmer (amplitude variation), harmonic-to-noise ratio, and nonlinear dynamics measures like PPE and DFA — and classify them using Support Vector Classifiers trained on clinical voice datasets.

NeuroTrace screens for four categories: HC (Healthy Control — no detected signs), PD (Parkinson's Disease), PSP (Progressive Supranuclear Palsy), and MSA (Multiple System Atrophy). PSP and MSA are rarer parkinsonian disorders that share some motor symptoms with PD but have distinct progression patterns.

Audio and image files are sent to the NeuroTrace API for processing and are not permanently stored — they are discarded immediately after inference. Survey responses are processed entirely in your browser and never leave your device. No personally identifiable information is collected or retained.

A positive or uncertain result from NeuroTrace is not a diagnosis — it is a signal to seek professional evaluation. Schedule an appointment with a neurologist, ideally one specializing in movement disorders. They can perform a comprehensive clinical assessment including physical examination, imaging, and other validated tests to determine whether further investigation is warranted.
